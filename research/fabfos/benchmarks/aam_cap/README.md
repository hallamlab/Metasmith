# Is the 600-atom cap a fact about the mappers, or about itself?

## The question

`aam.worklist.ATOM_LIMIT` refuses reactions over 600 atoms. Its warrant is a yield curve
joining reaction size to what the deployed bake banked: 98-99% up to 600, 12.8% from 600
to 800, 1.3% above, zero past 1,200.

**That curve is censored above the threshold, and the censoring is the cap itself.** In
the deployed `atom_pairs` table every mapper-derived method stops dead at 600 —
`localmapper_only` max 600, `indigo_only` 598, `rxnmapper_only` 563, `consensus` 581 —
because the same cut sat upstream of all three lanes. The only method that appears above
600 is `curated`, which comes from MetaCyc and never sees a mapper at all. So the
"12.8% banking rate" up there is MetaCyc's coverage, not a mapper's failure, and the
knee that justifies the constant is a wall the constant built.

A cap whose evidence is generated by the cap cannot be evaluated. This benchmark tests it
from the other side: hand the refused reactions to a mapper and see what happens.

## Why Indigo is the right instrument

The cap bounds two real costs and neither is Indigo's. RXNMapper's transformer accepts
512 tokens; LocalMapper is the lane that was OOM-killed twice, and its memory grows with
the string. Indigo is a compiled maximum-common-substructure search with a recorded
timeout — a different failure mode, and one the member lane already instruments.

So the cap is plausibly a **member boundary** rather than a property of the chemistry, and
that is the hypothesis under test.

## Running it

Needs a built `lookup::` directory (`buildlib::mnx_lookups build` from the MetaNetX
drop-in, minutes, disposable) and an adjudicated worklist. `epam-indigo==1.45.0` — the
`:aam` image's pin — is a pip install into `rdkit-scratch`.

    mamba run -n rdkit-scratch python research/fabfos/benchmarks/aam_cap/build_sets.py \
        --lookups <lookups> --worklist <worklist.parquet> --out <scratch>/sets.jsonl

    mamba run -n rdkit-scratch python research/fabfos/benchmarks/aam_cap/sweep.py \
        --sets <scratch>/sets.jsonl --budget 20 --workers 6 \
        --out <scratch>/results_20s.jsonl

    mamba run -n rdkit-scratch python research/fabfos/benchmarks/aam_cap/report.py \
        --results <scratch>/results_20s.jsonl \
        --outdir research/fabfos/benchmarks/aam_cap/results

231 reactions at a 20s budget is one to two hours, because the tail is hang-dominated
rather than slow. `sweep.py` appends and skips keys already recorded, so it resumes.

## What is known, and how well

**Verified, reproducible from what is on disk.** The censoring above. It needs only
`data/fabfos/processed/metabolism_bake/{atom_pairs,vocab}.parquet` and a per-reaction atom
count, and it is the finding the rest rests on.

**Measured once, raw results lost.** A sweep reached 64 of 231 reactions before the
session was OOM-killed and its scratch directory was cleaned. It read: **hit rate 0.266,
hang rate 0.359** on the size-refused whole reactions, and Indigo mapping a 1,666-atom
reaction in 1.8 seconds. Directionally clear and not yet a number to cite — `results/` is
empty on purpose, and the first job here is to re-run and fill it.

For scale, the lane's own record says Indigo times out on 51.5% of the 400-600 band, which
the cap **admits**. A ~27% hit rate above the cap is the same curve continuing, not a
cliff — which is what a censored threshold looks like from the far side.

## The hang, which is the blocker

Roughly a third of the refused reactions hang Indigo inside its compiled search, where
neither `signal.alarm` nor its own `aam-timeout` reaches. `sweep.py` answers that with a
subprocess per reaction and a parent that can `SIGKILL`; **the production lane cannot**.
`transforms/bake/indigo.py` backgrounds 28 shards and collects them with a bare `wait`
under a 12-hour duration, so a hung shard blocks until the lane's budget expires.

Routing oversized reactions into that lane as it stands trades 67 reactions for a stalled
member. The measurement is what says so, and it is why the routing change below is not
finished.

## The uncommitted change this belongs to

Nine files are modified in the working tree and none of it is committed. Two independent
pieces:

**The partial lane stopped measuring stoichiometric copies.** `reduce_for_element`
preserves stoichiometry, so its submissions were expansions — a phosphorus reduction of
nitrogenase is `16 ATP >> 16 ADP + 16 Pi`, over a thousand atoms of three molecules. It
now gets the second reading pass 1 gets, guarded by `partial._still_balances`: collapse is
taken only where the element still balances per copy, because otherwise the mapper is
handed three sources for five destinations and must invent which. Self-contained,
tested, and does not depend on anything above.

**The atom cap routes rather than refuses.** `worklist.NEURAL_ADMITS` / `INDIGO_ADMITS`
are read by both member modules, `oversize` rows carry their collapsed string, and the
partial lane emits over-cap submissions with the same verdict instead of dropping them.
A full re-adjudication confirms the neural universe does not move — 57,515 mappable, 454
recovered, 67 routed. **This piece is not safe to ship until the hang is contained.**

`tests/ecspr/bake` covers both: 121 passed, 2 skipped.

## Open problems, in the order they unblock each other

1. **Contain the hang.** Isolate over-cap attempts in `indigo_member` behind a subprocess
   with a hard kill, so a hang becomes a recorded outcome and costs its budget instead of
   the lane. Everything else in the routing change waits on this.
2. **Re-run the sweep** and fill `results/`. Rebuild the lookups, re-adjudicate, sweep all
   231 at the production budget. A second pass at a longer budget would also settle
   whether `indigo_member retry` is worth wiring back in — a question the lane has carried
   without a number.
3. **Regenerate the index with the source change.** `build_references/build.sh` vendors
   `src/ecspr` and recompiles the metadata; a source change committed without it is the
   stale-index bug in slow motion.
4. **R-group formulas, which are the bigger lever.** `count_element` returns None for any
   formula carrying `*`, and None propagates to refusing the whole reduction. That is
   **34.6%** of (reaction, element) slots on the size-refused set and **45.2%** on the
   >=900-char population — against roughly 7% lost to the atom cap. Every one of those
   participants has a structure. The sound rule is that an untrustworthy formula means
   "cannot prove this carries no X", so the participant is KEPT, which preserves the
   closure the reduction rests on; what is lost is the balance check, and the residue is
   usually conserved across the reaction, so the specified parts' balance may be the whole
   test. Needs its own soundness argument before any code.
5. **The 11 `too_long` reactions** are still refused everywhere, and correctly: their
   collapsed strings remain over the character cap, which is the bound that stops anything
   parsing MNXR144749's 80.7 MB. Recorded so a later reader does not re-open it.

## Read alongside

`../aam_collapse/` measures the stoichiometric collapse and holds the yield curve this
benchmark shows to be censored above 600. Its `yield_curve.tsv` is still correct below
the threshold and still the warrant for the constant there; what changes is that the rows
above 600 describe MetaCyc rather than a mapper.
